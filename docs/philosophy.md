# Project Philosophy

Code Review Harness is built around a small number of durable principles.

## Evidence over opinion

Assertions must be grounded in attributable evidence. Evidence quality, freshness, completeness, and conflicts are part of the assessment rather than inconvenient footnotes.

## Immutable source artifacts

Review outputs may interpret and correlate upstream artifacts, but they do not silently rewrite them. Corrections produce new versioned artifacts with explicit lineage.

## Human decision authority

Agents may analyze, score, recommend, compare, and escalate. Humans approve releases, accept risk, modify requirements, grant exceptions, select alternatives, and authorize promotion.

## Traceability by default

Findings, risks, CAPAs, requirements, evidence, architecture decisions, and human decisions must remain connected through stable identifiers and bidirectional references.

## Explainable assessment

Scores and conclusions must expose methods, rubrics, assumptions, weighting, normalization, uncertainty, and disagreement. Unexplained averaging is not synthesis.

## Corrective and preventive action

Material findings should lead to corrective and preventive action appropriate to their cause, consequence, and recurrence risk. Closure requires evidence and authorized human disposition.

## Confidence is not certainty

Evidence confidence, assessment confidence, and decision confidence are distinct. Confidence provenance must be preserved, and weak or conflicting evidence must remain visible.

## Bounded automation

Automation should reduce repetitive work without erasing accountability. Agent evolution occurs in controlled sandboxes, is measured against representative benchmarks, and requires human approval before promotion.

## Reviewability is a design requirement

Artifacts, workflows, and changes should be understandable, reproducible, and reviewable by someone other than their original author. Complexity without traceability is merely confusion wearing a badge.
